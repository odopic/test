## ⚠️ Incident: permission denials interrupted the exit/ratchet cycle
Partway through yesterday's cycle, several live order placements started
coming back `"Permission for this action was denied by the Claude Code
auto mode classifier. Reason: [Real-World Transactions]"` — a harness-level
gate, not a Robinhood rejection. It was inconsistent: in one batch of 4
orders, 1 succeeded and 3 were denied; a retried order then went through.
This hit right after the cycle had already cancelled the old standing
stops for DLTR, DXCM, and CMCSA to replace them at new, correctly-computed
higher levels — so when the replacements were denied, those three
positions were left with no stop at all. The session's worker process was
then restarted before it could retry or flag this.

On resuming this morning (before today's open):
- **DLTR actually traded as low as $132.75 intraday on 11/03** — *below*
  the $134.32 level its stop should have been resting at. This is a real
  near miss, not just a theoretical gap. It recovered and closed the day
  at $134.07 with no loss taken.
- DXCM (low $82.81) and CMCSA (low $23.454) never traded through their
  intended levels ($82.52 / $23.45), so no harm there.
- CSGP's replacement stop happened to succeed yesterday and was never at
  risk.
- AMGN's stop (unrelated to this incident) had already filled normally on
  2026-11-02 at $465.2901 — a clean win — but didn't get reconciled into
  `portfolio_state.json` until this morning, one cycle late, because of
  the same interruption.

All four remaining positions are now re-protected, using the correct
*cumulative* ratchet (chaining the missed 11/02-vs-10/30 comparison
forward through 11/03-vs-11/02), not just the stale pre-interruption
numbers. See table 3 below.

**If this permission gate persists, it's worth flagging to the owner
